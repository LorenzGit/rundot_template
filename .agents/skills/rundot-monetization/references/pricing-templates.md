# RUN pricing templates

Default RB price points that sell well. RB = RUN Bits (platform hard currency,
bought with real money; ~100 RB ≈ $1 USD — verify against the live catalog).

**Observed on the live Run Bits purchase screen, 2026-07-27.** The base rate
holds across every non-bonus tier; the two largest packs carry their advertised
bonus. Re-verify before pricing — a dated observation, not a guarantee.

| Bundle | Price | RB per $ | Bonus |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 200 RB | $1.99 | 100.5 | — |
| 500 RB | $4.99 | 100.2 | — |
| 1,000 RB | $9.99 | 100.1 | — |
| 2,000 RB | $19.99 | 100.1 | — |
| 6,000 RB | $49.99 | 120.0 | +20% |
| 13,000 RB | $99.99 | 130.0 | +30% |

**A price band is not a unit.** A game priced at 99/199/299/399/499 RB was once
flagged as tenfold underpriced because sibling titles sold packs at 400–4,000
RB. That was wrong: those siblings are *expensive products* — a 4,000 RB pack is
a $40 purchase — not a different currency scale. The original ladder was already
correct at $0.99–$4.99. A wide spread across comparables means the games differ,
not the currency; check the rate rather than deriving it from other prices.
"Premium currency" = your game's own currency (e.g. gems), bought with RB and
spent in-game.

Adjust to your game, but start here rather than guessing — and remember the
overarching rule: **don't underprice.**

## Premium currency packs (the revenue backbone)

Six tiers. Higher tiers give more "free" currency via a growing bonus %, which
pushes players toward bigger packs. Show the bonus on each pack so the value is
obvious.

| RB price | Premium currency | Bonus |
|---------:|-----------------:|------:|
|      400 |              250 |   —   |
|      800 |              600 | +20%  |
|    1,600 |            1,500 | +50%  |
|    2,400 |            2,500 | +67%  |
|    4,000 |            5,000 | +100% |
|    8,000 |           12,500 | +150% |

Make sure there is something worth buying with the currency — permanent unlocks
(stat cards, new buildings/relics, characters) give the best perceived value.

## First-purchase deal (one-time, highly recommended)

Apply once, to the first premium-currency pack a player buys, then revert to
normal. Two common forms:

1. **Discounted price** on the first pack purchased, or
2. **2× currency** on the first pack purchased. E.g. a player buys the 1,600 RB
   tier and receives **3,000** premium currency instead of 1,500. All later
   packs are normal.

Form 2 (2× currency) is the simplest high-impact version.

## Bundles

A bundle = stat boosts + some premium currency, sold as one item. Price it so
the **included premium currency alone would cost about the same** as the bundle
— then the boosts read as free, so it looks like (and is) a smart buy.

Worked example:

- Bundle price: **600 RB**
- Includes: **250 gems** + a stat boost + a unique unlock (e.g. a 3× speed perk)
- 250 gems bought on their own = **400 RB**
- → the player effectively gets the boosts + unique unlock for ~200 RB. Great deal.

## Ice-breaker offer

The cheapest deliberate "first spend" nudge.

- Price: **~100 RB** (~$1)
- **Limited-time** (e.g. a 22–24h countdown; it disappears after).
- **Visually distinct** (different color from other shop items).
- You can run several different ice-breakers, or re-show the same one, if the
  player doesn't bite. Mildly annoying, but effective.

## Depth-of-spend tier (gated, premium)

A higher-priced bundle aimed at players who have already bought in. Gate it
behind a prior purchase so only invested players see it.

Worked example:

- A premium "tier 2" bundle — price **1,400 RB**, with premium styling (e.g. a
  glowing/magenta treatment).
- Gated until the player has bought the entry bundle above.
- Packs bigger perks: a larger gameplay boost + a generous currency grant
  (e.g. +750 gems).

The rule: someone who unlocked the prerequisite is someone who likes the game
and is likely to buy more — so make this bundle genuinely great.

## Sanity check before launch

Pick a successful, popular game that's a real comp for yours. Look at what IAPs
it offers and what they cost. If your prices are far below the comp, you're
probably leaving money on the table.
